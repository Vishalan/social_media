Shorts Pipeline

One input — a link, pasted prose, or a topic to research — becomes a finished vertical short. Everything runs on the GPU server; no metered API is in the path.

  • network
  • claude -p
  • GPU (exclusive)
  • CPU / ffmpeg

The one rule that matters

Three services share one 24 GB card and none of them owns it. Generation needs 21.7 GB, so it cannot coexist with the voice model or Whisper. Every stage clears the others on the way in and releases on the way out.

The reason this is stated as a rule rather than left to chance: the failure never says “out of memory”. ComfyUI reports it as {"status_str": "error"}, the director logs a warning, drops the clip, and the video ships one shot short with nothing explaining why. A second symptom was an HTTP 500 whose real cause was visible only inside another container’s log.

Note also that PyTorch keeps its allocator reservation after models unload, so nvidia-smi still shows ~22 GB held. Harmless for the process that owns the pool; fatal for anything else.

Modules

One responsibility each. The split that matters most is decide versus make versus assemble: the director chooses and produces payloads but renders nothing itself, the generators know one medium each and nothing about the story, and the assembler knows geometry and time but never content.

The two branding layers, kept apart

sourcebrand borrows the subject’s identity so each story looks like it came from that story. branding holds the channel’s constants so a viewer recognises the video as yours. Collapsing them is how a video about X ended up branded as TechCrunch.

B-roll kinds

Two groups, and the distinction drives the scene floor: a scene puts imagery or a shot on screen, a card puts type on a rectangle. At least half of every slate must be scenes, enforced in code because the rule stated in the prompt alone was ignored.

stock_clipThe subject is physically filmable — a place, an object, an activityseconds

Layout

Three span modes, alternating so the edit never settles into one shape. Full-frame graphics may take up to 55% of the running time.

  • content

    Graphic in the upper panel, presenter in the lower half. The default.

  • content_full

    Graphic owns the whole frame; the presenter is absent for its duration.

  • content_pip

    Full-frame graphic with the presenter in a round corner PIP, alternating sides. Keeps a person on screen without giving up the frame.

Invariants

Each of these exists because its absence shipped in a finished video.

  • legibility is a pair, not a colour

    Text colours are chosen by contrast ratio against their actual background. Absolute brightness once picked a subject’s background as its own accent — a headline rendered on itself.

  • a page that hides its text is not shown

    If fetching a page did not yield the article, a screenshot of it is not evidence — it is the paywall. A Bloomberg bot check once ran as b-roll.

  • fit, never assume

    Every text block is measured against the box that holds it. Estimated advance ratios put code off the panel edge and headlines through their own kicker.

  • the presenter is never full-frame

    A span with no panel content used to fall through to a full-frame avatar — silently, on seven spans of eleven.

  • layout decides before the renderer

    Render size follows the span decision. Sizing from eligibility instead meant 1920-tall clips dropped into a 998 panel, cropped rather than fitted.

  • the subject, not the publisher

    Designed panels wear the subject’s mark. Quote beds keep the publisher’s, because they are quoting it.

Running it

commandresult
/short <url>Fetches, falls back to any prose pasted with it
/short <topic> --researchSearches the web first
/short … --avatarRenders the lip-synced presenter (+~35 min)

Without --avatar the lower half is a black hold. That is the fast path — roughly 8–12 minutes instead of 40–50 — and it is the right default while iterating on voice and b-roll.
